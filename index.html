Addresses Worth Knowing

Your saved Google Maps pins, plus picks from Condé Nast Traveler, Monocle, Michelin, Paris by Mouth & the Paris bakers' guild.

From Your Saved Map

La Tour Montlhéry — Chez Denise · 5 Rue des Prouvaires, 1st · minutes from the hotel
Old-school Les Halles institution serving until midnight — beef cheeks, escargot, generous everything. Reserve ahead; it fills fast. A natural welcome-dinner pick.
Montezuma Café · 15 Rue Notre Dame des Victoires, 2nd
Natural wine bar with great music, a short walk from the hotel. Opens 6 PM; closed Sun–Mon.
Terroirs d'Avenir · 3 Rue du Nil, 2nd
Standout almond croissants on one of the city's great little food streets — the same block as Frenchie. Closed Monday.
The bouillons · Julien (10th) · Chartier Grands Boulevards (9th) · Racine (6th) · Chartier Montparnasse (14th)
Grand belle-époque dining halls, hearty classics at gentle prices. Julien's art-nouveau room is the showstopper. Most don't take reservations — go before 7 or after 9 to dodge the queue.
Ambassade du Texas 1842–1843 · 1 Place Vendôme, 1st
The plaque marking where the Republic of Texas kept its Paris embassy — high on the façade, easiest spotted with a zoom lens. Five minutes from the hotel, right on the shopping route.
Mamiche · 45 Rue Condorcet, 9th  ·  The French Bastards · several locations
Mamiche sits on the walk up to Montmartre (closed Sun–Mon); The French Bastards do big, indulgent modern pastries.
Levain, Le Vin · 83 Rue du Faubourg Saint-Martin, 10th
A 4.9-star sliver of a wine bar — house-baked bread, superb charcuterie, an owner who pairs the wine for you. Closed Sunday.
Le Machon d'Henri · 8 Rue Guisarde, 6th  ·  Poilâne · 8 Rue du Cherche-Midi, 6th
A tiny Saint-Germain bistro (best for four or fewer; reserve) and the legendary sourdough bakery — both pair perfectly with a Le Bon Marché shopping run.
Linette · 16 Av. Rapp, 7th
Crowd-pleasing French classics — onion soup, duck confit, steak tartare — right by the Eiffel Tower if a visit ends up on the agenda.
Place des Vosges · Le Marais
Paris's oldest square, arcaded and lovely — an easy anchor for a Marais wander on the free day.

Steps From the Hotel

Brasserie du Louvre · inside your hotel
A Paul Bocuse brasserie doing the classics — onion soup, escargot, sole meunière — with a terrace facing the Palais-Royal. The easiest great meal of the trip.
La Poule au Pot · 9 Rue Vauvilliers, 1st
Jean-François Piège's beloved old-school bistro; a shrine to French classics a few minutes' walk away.
Loulou · Musée des Arts Décoratifs, 1st
One of the prettiest terraces in Paris, looking over the Tuileries — made for a golden-hour dinner.
Verjus · behind the Palais-Royal, 1st
Seasonal tasting menu with standout wine pairings; takes private groups of 8–12 — a strong candidate for the dinner with friends.
Willi's Wine Bar · 13 Rue des Petits-Champs, 1st
An institution since 1980; easygoing, great with larger tables.
La Bourse et la Vie · 2nd
Celebration-worthy classic bistro with one of the city's best steak frites.

Up in Montmartre

Le Moulin de la Galette · 18th
Dining beneath the windmill Renoir painted — history with lunch.
Le Bon et la Butte · 18th
A neighborhood favorite locals return to for the duck confit.
Mokko / a.lea / Ose · 18th
Michelin's current Montmartre picks — small, modern, market-driven rooms below the basilica.
Skip Place du Tertre's cafés
Lovely square, forgettable food — walk two streets in any direction instead.

Boulangerie Detours

Boulangerie du Sentier · 2nd, walkable from the hotel
Crowned best butter croissant in greater Paris for 2026 by the bakers' guild.
Le Fournil Didot · 103 Rue Didot, 14th
Winner of the 2026 Grand Prix for the best baguette in Paris — worth the detour if you're on the Left Bank.
Du Pain et des Idées · 10th  ·  Blé Sucré · 12th
Two perennial favorites: a gorgeous 19th-century shop, and what many critics call the city's most refined croissant.
Le Grenier à Pain · 38 Rue des Abbesses, 18th
A past best-baguette winner conveniently on the Montmartre route.

For the Shopping Day

Rue Saint-Honoré · out the hotel door
Heritage maisons and newer labels without the Champs-Élysées crush — look for Moynat's handbags and Astier de Villatte's handmade ceramics among the big names.
Place Vendôme & Rue de la Paix · 1st/2nd
The jewelers' square — Cartier, Boucheron, Piaget — five minutes from the hotel.
Le Bon Marché · 7th
Paris's oldest department store and still its most elegantly edited.
Galeries Lafayette Haussmann · 9th
Worth a visit for the stained-glass dome alone.

From Condé Nast Traveler's 2026 List

Allard · 6th
The magazine's nod to enduring bistro tradition on the Left Bank.

Two of the buzzier names on this year's list — creative French cooking, the latter with Asian influences.

Galway, 45 Minutes South

The Crane Bar · 2 Sea Road, Galway's West End
Your pin — one of Ireland's great trad-music pubs. The room goes quiet for the musicians, which is the whole point. Music from mid-afternoon most days.
Aniar · 53 Lower Dominick Street
Galway's Michelin-starred table — a tasting menu built entirely from the West of Ireland's larder. Book well ahead if a Galway evening materializes.
Ard Bia at Nimmos · by the Spanish Arch
The city's most iconic casual table, rustic and riverside — ideal for lunch on a day trip.
Kai · Sea Road, two doors from The Crane
Beloved seasonal cooking in the West End — lunch here, music next door after.
Tigh Neachtain · Quay Street
A 19th-century pub at the heart of the Latin Quarter, painted that famous blue — the classic Galway pint stop.

Dublin

Your Picks, Confirmed

Guinness Storehouse · St. James's Gate
On the schedule for arrival day, Oct 24. Pre-book timed tickets; the Gravity Bar pint at the top is included.
FIRE Steakhouse · The Mansion House, Dawson Street
Wood-fired steaks and seafood in a grand room beside the Lord Mayor's residence. Penciled in for the Oct 24 group dinner — reserve early, tables are hard to get.
O'Donoghue's · 15 Merrion Row · beside the Shelbourne
You had it right — steps from St Stephen's Green. The pub where The Dubliners got their start; live trad most nights and Sunday afternoons. Don't confuse it with the other O'Donoghue's on Suffolk Street.
O'Neill's Pub & Kitchen · 2 Suffolk Street
A warren of snugs near Trinity, famous for its carvery — order at the bar, find a corner. Better for lunch and stew than a fancy night.

Worth Adding

Kevin & Howlin · 31 Nassau Street · across from Trinity
Dublin's Donegal tweed institution — jackets, caps, waistcoats, and made-to-measure suits. Note the hours: 10:30 AM – 4:30 PM, closed Sundays, so plan it for Monday or Tuesday.
Chapter One · Parnell Square
Dublin's two-Michelin-star standard-bearer under chef Mickael Viljanen — the city's blowout meal if one is wanted.
Glovers Alley · The Fitzwilliam Hotel, St Stephen's Green
Elegant Michelin-listed dining a short walk around the Green from the Shelbourne. The lunch menu is notably good value.
D'Olier Street · opposite Trinity College
A Michelin star within two years of opening; precise, ingredient-led cooking in a Victorian corner room.
Uno Mas · Aungier Street
Spanish tapas with Irish produce — a Condé Nast Traveller Dublin pick, relaxed enough for a no-plans evening.
Bread 41 · Pearse Street
The bakery on every Dublin best-of list — the morning pastry stop before the flight home.

New Since Your Last Visit · All Walkable

Etto · 18 Merrion Row · beside O'Donoghue's
Tiny, stylish, Italian-leaning, in the Michelin guide and great value for the quality — right on your block. Bookings open 60 days ahead and it fills fast; this is the one to reserve early.
9 Below · 9 St Stephen's Green · across the Green
A discreet Georgian-basement cocktail bar in the old Hibernian Club — arched ceilings, rare-whiskey cabinet, a tight list of beautifully built drinks. Closed Mon–Tues; the kind of hidden room you'd never find without being told.
The Ivy Asia · near Trinity / Dawson Street
The Ivy Group's first international opening — opulent pan-Asian, more night-out than quiet dinner. New in 2026 and drawing crowds.
Comet · King Street South, St Stephen's Green
A 2026 opening from chef Joshua Lane, moments off the Green — one of the freshest tables in the neighbourhood.
Laszlo's · Arthaus Hotel, Mercer Street
A handsome new cocktail bar — green booths, a big mural, premium-spirit drinks — a few minutes west of the Green.
Sophie's at The Dean · Harcourt Street rooftop
Glass-walled rooftop with near-360° views over the city and the mountains, wood-fired pizzas and cocktails. (Rooftop terrace reopened spring 2026.) A short stroll south-west.
Mr. Fox · Parnell Square  ·  Amai by Viktor · above The Corkscrew
Two of the city's most-talked-about rooms if you fancy a proper dinner out — Mr. Fox for inventive contemporary Irish, Amai a buzzy Michelin-listed newcomer tipped for a star.
